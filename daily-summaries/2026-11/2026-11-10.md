# Daily Summary - 2026-11-09

## Key highlights
- A caching engine modeled after LMCache and SGLang with reduced write-through supports distributed offloading, load-balanced setups, and hot/warm/cool/cold tiers across multi-machine and multi-instance deployments. A DRAM tier is recommended to avoid NVMe write churn; one report said 8-16 GB is usually plenty and another advised always including a 4 GB or 8 GB DRAM tier and avoiding direct VRAM-to-NVMe caching due to row write size and row expiry. A production setup was reported as VRAM -> 128 GB DRAM -> 2 TB local NVMe -> 40 TB SAN NVMe. - Flare TP3 agent-traffic replay (3x RTX PRO 6000 Max-Q, 300W, DS4.1-Flash) did not show decode-bench gains: at 4/8 streams median TTFT rose to 2.00/3.52s vs 0.43/0.42s on vLLM; new-turn prefill waited 2-3s. Cause: Flare's scheduler values an output token as 64 prompt tokens, so active decode outbids prefill until fairness weight builds; vLLM folds prompts into the next decode step. Prefill-priority work is planned. HOT=300 OOM at 95.03GiB; HOT=290 export worked. ## Benchmarks and implementation findings

[(1)](https://discord.com/channels/1466898002793857221/1471527895439638528/1569262160507441182) [(2)](https://discord.com/channels/1466898002793857221/1471527895439638528/1569262542478377020)
