## What the change does

`VLLM_PLE_TABLE_MEMORY=shared` keeps the b12x `mapped_host` policy but puts
the bytes in `/dev/shm/vllm-ple/<key>/{weight.bin,weight_scale.bin}`. The
key `6ee28885c99ed11e` is the first 16 hex digits of a SHA-256 over model
path, revision, quant mode, TP size/rank, PLE layer ordinal, padded vocabulary,
shard range, embedding dim and both tensors' shapes and dtypes. The first
process to take `<key>.lock` (`flock`) creates the files, maps them
`MAP_SHARED`, registers them with `cudaHostRegister`, fills them through the
unchanged checkpoint loader, and once `_validate_embedding_loaded` proves
every local row and scale row covered it `msync`s, writes `manifest.json`,
creates `READY` and releases the lock. Later processes attach: they wait on
the lock, check every manifest field against their own plan, map and register
the same files, start with their load ranges complete, and skip the shard
copies. Their model-level `checkpoint_file_weight_filter` routes the 128 shard
names through the loader's existing file-backed path, so the payloads are not
read. `weight_scale_2` and the three hash-geometry buffers still come from the
checkpoint in every process. b12x is unchanged.

## Startup

Pod start (`status.startTime`) to `Ready`, 10 s probe granularity; engine
timings from the logs.

| Run | To Ready | `Loading weights` | `Model loading` | `init engine` | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| ram baseline, first start after the 27B left the GPU | 330 s | 244.3 s | 259.3 s | 29.2 s | cold ZFS ARC |
| ram baseline, second start | 351 s | 271.8 s | 287.5 s | 31.1 s | warm ARC, warm JIT cache |
| shared, populate (first ever) | 251 s | 31.8 s | 52.3 s | 136.5 s (27.9 s compile) | `Populated … after 69.9s`; fresh JIT cache directory |
| shared, populate after the engine was killed mid-populate | 121 s | — | — | — | `Populated … after 47.0s`, warm JIT |
| shared, attach | 111 s | 27.5 s | 40.9 s | 36.9 s (3.9 s compile) | table already in tmpfs |
| shared, attach (second) | 101 s | 23.4 s | 36.0 s | 31.6 s | |

The ram mode's weight loading is slow in both cold and warm runs. The shared
mapping is ordinary cacheable memory, while the b12x `ram` table is
`cudaHostAllocWriteCombined`; the loader's per-shard finite/positive scale
check reads the 3 GB scale plane back on the CPU, which is very slow from
write-combined memory. That, not disk, dominates the 244–272 s. Populate and
attach load the same 75 GiB of model weights in 23–32 s.

Attachers avoid the shard payload reads (file-backed filter) but the saving
is small here because the checkpoint was in the ARC; it will matter more on a
cold host. A reboot clears `/dev/shm`; the first replica repopulates, so the
startup probe budget is 40 min in the manifest.

## Memory

| Where | ram baseline | shared, populating replica | shared, attached replica |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Pod cgroup `memory.current` | 5.0 GiB | 33.1 GiB (`shmem` 27.5 GiB) | 7.6–7.9 GiB (`shmem` 36 MiB) |
| Host `free` "shared" | 1 GiB | 28 GiB | 28 GiB (unchanged) |
| Host `free` "used" delta on start | +28 GiB (cold run), +56 GiB (warm run; includes ARC growth) | +32 GiB | +13 GiB |
| VRAM used | 94,981 MiB | 94,523 MiB | 94,523 MiB |
| Available KV cache | 7.98 GiB | 7.51 GiB | 7.53 GiB |

The `cudaHostAlloc` table of the ram mode is driver memory: it never shows in
the pod's cgroup, so Kubernetes accounting under-reports that mode by 26.82
GiB per replica. tmpfs pages are charged to the cgroup that first touches them
(the populator, 27.5 GiB `shmem`), persist after that pod exits, and cost an
attacher nothing beyond page tables. The 80Gi limit therefore stays on every
Deployment (any replica may populate after a reboot) while requests can be
sized from the 7.9 GiB attacher footprint; the manifests use 24Gi.

The 0.45 GiB lower KV budget in shared mode is reproducible across the two
attach starts; its cause was not isolated (the profiling run, not the table,
decides it). It is noted, not explained.

## Serving throughput, same GPU, same day

Three 30 s measurements per cell after 10 s warm-up, context 0, temperature 1,
EOS respected; medians in bold, individual runs in brackets.

|---|---:|---:|---:|

| C8 | **627.6** [616.3, 627.8, 627.6] | **615.1** [608.6, 633.9, 615.1] | −2.0 % |
| C16 | **603.9** [603.9, 661.3, 575.6] | **644.7** [663.8, 638.4, 644.7] | +6.8 % |
| Uncached 32K prefill (client, 24 samples) | **13,207** (TTFT 2.435 s) | **13,460** (TTFT 2.389 s) | +1.9 % |

All deltas sit inside the baseline's own run-to-run spread (C1 ±5 %, C16

a 12 s CUDA unit-test execution inside the same pod; its C1 (175.7) is not an
outlier. C16 is well below the recipe's idle-host 944 tok/s in both modes
because of the concurrent production load and the canary's 6,019-token batch
budget; that is a property of this window, not of the table mode.

| Check | Result |
|---|---|

## Scheduler, memory and TP2 tuning (same day, same image)

After the shared-table qualification, the serving profile itself was swept on
the same GPU with the same benchmark ([receipts](shared-ple-r35-20260916-tuning.json)
and `shared-ple-r35-20260916-tuning-*.json`). One measurement per cell unless
bracketed; medians where two runs exist; production load on the other GPUs
throughout. "Small jobs" = C128 offered, 256-token outputs, 60 s.

| Profile | seqs | MTP | KV | graphs | C1 | C16 | C32 | C64 | C128 | small jobs tok/s / done per min / TTFT p50 | running cap |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| A canary | 16 | 3 | 7.96 GiB (0.90) | 64 | **177** | 644 | 635 | 650 | 632 | 661 / 305 / 45 s | 16 |
| B | 128 | 3 | 4.2 GiB (0.88) | 512 | 173 | 481 | 490 | 477 | — | 474 / 220 / 60 s | 4 |
| C | 128 | off | 8.27 GiB (0.88) | 128 | 121 | **922** | 888 | 895 | 889 | 919 / 425 / 29 s | 16–17 |
| D | 128 | off | 13 GiB explicit | 128 | 120 | — | OOM | — | — | — | ~28, crashed |
| F | 128 | off | 11.5 GiB explicit | 32 | 120 | — | **1,024** | **1,026** | — | **1,128 / 509 / 22 s** | 24 |

Findings:

- **Concurrency is KV-bound, not `--max-num-seqs`-bound.** A fresh request
  costs ~5.5 % of C's 629K-token cache before generating a token (the 36
  GDN layers' recurrent state, stored in the same block pool and padded to
  the 3008-token attention page), i.e. ≈0.45 GiB per request. Running
  requests ≈ KV GiB ÷ 0.45; everything above that queues. Throughput scales
  with that cap because the GPU is neither compute- nor power-bound (≈300 W
  of 500 W at C16).
- **MTP3 costs ~30 % of batch throughput** (A→C at C16) and ≈2 GiB of VRAM
  (1.49 GiB draft module + NVFP4 LM-head copy + speculator graphs); it gives
  +47 % single-stream (177 vs 121). B shows MTP plus a large batch is the
  worst of both: the draft slots shrink the KV to 4.2 GiB and only 4
  requests run.
- **`--gpu-memory-utilization` is the wrong knob here; use
  `--kv-cache-memory`.** vLLM's own accounting (74.15 GiB weights+non-torch,
  1.15 GiB activation, 0.29 GiB graphs) misses ≈6 GiB of real usage on this
  driver, so its "fully utilize" suggestion (18.7 GiB) is unsafe: 13 GiB OOMs
  in the GDN forward at C32 (`torch.OutOfMemoryError`, 76 MiB free).
  11.5 GiB survives with ≈2 GB free under load.
- **Fewer CUDA graphs are not a lever**: capping capture at 32 instead of 128
  frees ≈0.37 GiB.

### TP2 versus two TP1 replicas (F1:00.0 x16 + eGPU 01:00.0 x4, no P2P)

With qwen3-8-27b scaled to zero the same two GPUs ran one TP2 instance
(`--tensor-parallel-size 2`, B12X PCIe all-reduce, `NCCL_P2P_DISABLE=1`, no
MTP, `gpu-memory-utilization 0.88`; each rank attached its own 13.41 GiB
shared table, populated in 57 s) and, alternately, two TP1 replicas of
profile F attached to the one 26.82 GiB table (the second attacher added no
host memory: `shared` stayed at 55 GiB).

| | 2 × TP1 (F) | TP2, 128 seqs | TP2, 256 seqs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| KV per GPU | 11.5 GiB (875K tok) | 43.8 GiB (6.32M tok) | 43.8 GiB (6.24M tok) |
| C1 | 120 | 133 | — |
| C32 total | 1,844 (2 × C16) | 1,565 | — |
| C64 total | 2,048 (2 × C32) | 2,228 | — |
| C128 | ≈2,050 (2 × 24 running) | 2,706 | 2,690 |
| C192 / C256 | — | — | **3,026 / 3,028** (191 running, KV 99.6 %) |
| small jobs | ≈2 × F | 2,247 tok/s, 512/min at C128, TTFT 0.2 s | 2,312 tok/s, 955/min at C256, TTFT p50 6 s |

TP2 delivers ≈**+48 % per GPU** for long-generation batch work (1,515 vs
1,026 tok/s per GPU) and does not queue below ~190 requests; for short jobs
the two are within noise of each other; single-stream both trail the MTP
canary. The upstream `tp2` recipe profile (MTP3, 16 sequences, 0.96
utilization, private PLE per rank, "requires working GPU peer access") is a
different configuration and was not measured. One repeat per cell; an x16
pair should not do worse than this x16 + x4 pair.

## Not covered

- The eGPU replica was only exercised inside the TP2 / 2 × TP1 tuning runs
  above (both attached the shared table and served), not with the full
